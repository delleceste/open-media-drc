Subject: Bit-perfect hi-fi on FreeBSD: what it found in uaudio(4), and a question for you

Hi Christos,

You committed 755685dd665e ("snd_uaudio: Don't let an idle stream reprogram a
shared UAC2 clock") in July via PR #2323. That bug did not come from code
reading -- it came from trying to do something with FreeBSD's sound stack that I
do not think anyone has seriously tried before, and I would like to tell you
about it, because I think the workload is worth more to you than the patch was.


THE WORKLOAD

I run a project called Open Media DRC: a FreeBSD music system aiming at
bit-perfect playback with digital room correction.

  MPD -> brutefir (DRC) -> virtual_oss -> OKTO DAC8 Stereo (USB)
      -> Apollon Purifi 1ET9040BA -> B&W Nautilus 801

  https://github.com/delleceste/open-media-drc

FreeBSD's USB audio stack is, as far as I can tell, mostly exercised by headsets,
webcams and MIDI interfaces. This is a different animal, and it lights up paths
that essentially nothing else does:

  - UAC2 devices with a single Clock Source shared between both directions
  - 44.1k <-> 48k crystal-family switches, on every track change, all day
  - rates to 384 kHz, 32-bit
  - explicit isochronous feedback endpoints
  - hours-long uninterrupted streams
  - and bit-perfectness verified end to end, so corruption is detectable
    rather than a matter of opinion

That last point is what makes it useful as a test surface. When something is
wrong here, it is provably wrong.

So far this workload has produced one commit you already took, four more
uaudio(4) defects that are still open, and separate bugs in virtual_oss
(a cuse teardown deadlock and a SETTRIGGER livelock). I am not short of
material. The question is whether it is useful to you.


WHAT IS STILL OPEN

PR #2390, opened 26 August, three commits. Still open, no reviewer assigned:

  https://github.com/freebsd/freebsd-src/pull/2390

The one I would most like a second opinion on is the first, because it is the
one 755685dd665e was meant to prevent and does not. The guard skips a write only
on a rate mismatch:

    if (other != 0 && other != chan_alt->sample_rate)   /* skip */

but the rate alignment added by the same commit (uaudio_chan_match_rate() in
uaudio_chan_start()) guarantees a match. The capture pass always falls through
and writes the shared clock -- after the playback alternate setting has already
armed the device.

I checked main this morning: uaudio20_clock_is_shared() and
uaudio_chan_match_rate() are in, everything else is absent, and
uaudio_chan_need_both() is unchanged. All four defects are live.


THE EVIDENCE, TIGHTENED

I had previously shown this with hw.usb.uaudio.debug=6, which is only a driver
log line. I have now traced usbd_do_request_flags() with DTrace, decoding
bmRequestType/bRequest/wValue, so what is recorded is the request that actually
went on the wire. Three open/play/close cycles of digital silence -- deliberately
silence, so the result says nothing about audibility and cannot be dismissed
on those grounds.

                                    before    after
  SET_CUR per start, same rate         2        0
  SET_CUR per start, rate change       2        1
  SET_CUR after playback armed         1        0
  SET_CUR during playback              0        0
  capture stream armed              every       never

Two results I did not expect, both of which cut against my own patches:

  - prefer_feedback is safe on this device, with evidence rather than hope.
    The risk was a device declaring UE_ISO_USAGE_FEEDBACK without honouring it.
    With the capture stream gone, dev.pcm.0.feedback_rate reads 44101 and 48001:
    the endpoint is live and tracking.

  - The stock feedback clamp never saturates here -- about 1 Hz offset, ~23 ppm,
    against howmany(44100, 16000) = +-3 Hz. I had a further local patch to
    replace that clamped correction with a smooth one. On this DAC it is not
    needed, so I am not proposing it.

Script, logs and method are in the repo under freebsd-uaudio-patch/traces/.
Reproducing needs nothing but DTrace.

Rebase note: 5c3bc8a (21 Aug) rewrote uaudio20_set_speed() to uDWord/USETDW().
Commit 1/3 adds uaudio20_get_speed() right after it in the old style, so expect
a conflict there. Say the word and I will rebase before you look.


THE PART I AM LEAST SURE ABOUT

Here is the honest bit, and the reason I am writing to you rather than just
pinging the PR.

The bits are provably identical on FreeBSD -- I have verified that. And yet my
subjective impression is that the same chain sounds better under Linux.

I know exactly how that sounds. It is not level-matched, not blind, not
simultaneous, and expectation bias is the single most likely explanation. I am
not claiming a defect, and I would rather be wrong.

But since the bits are identical, anything real has to be electrical or in
timing, and there I would trust your judgement over my ears. What I have found
so far that is FreeBSD-specific and real:

  - the redundant SET_CUR above, landing after the stream is armed. The trace
    bounds this: it is a transient at stream start, not continuous.

  - the auto-started capture stream. On this DAC it is a phantom -- the input
    terminal is declared as a Microphone (wTerminalType 0x0201) on a converter
    with no ADC. uaudio(4) streams it for the entire duration of every playback,
    about 126 isochronous transfers a second inbound from a device that has
    nothing to send, purely to recompute a number the feedback endpoint already
    reports. Linux never starts it. This one is continuous, and it is the one I
    find hardest to dismiss.

Neither is proven audible. Both are unnecessary work, and both are gone in the
PR.
