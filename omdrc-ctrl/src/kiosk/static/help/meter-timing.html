Meter timing

How the level meters and the spectrum are lined up with the sound you hear, and what the calibration does.

The problem

The meters are computed from a copy of the audio that the box takes before the audio reaches the speakers. Between that copy (the tap) and your ears the sound still has to pass through the room-correction filter, BruteFIR's buffers and the DAC's buffer. The meter frames go a different way: over the network to this screen, which then has to draw them. Neither path takes the same time, so without correction the bars flick before or after the sound.

The box's part: hold back what it can measure

The box knows some of the sound path exactly, and holds each frame back by that much before sending it:

StageHow it is known
Filter group delaythe peak of the active filter's impulse response
BruteFIR partitionfilter_length of the running configuration, at its rate
DAC buffer, DRC offread live from the kernel: ALSA's delay on Linux, the channel's buffer fill (sndctl) on FreeBSD

It then subtracts a margin (150 ms to start with), so frames always leave a little early. The rest of the path (buffers nothing reports, the USB link, the network, the screen) is not guessed. Each screen measures it for itself.

The screen's part: wait the rest

A frame reaches this screen some time after it leaves the box. The screen then waits its own extra display delay before drawing it. That delay is what the calibration finds: the time between a frame arriving and its sound being heard.

A screen can only wait. It cannot draw a frame before it has arrived. That is why the box errs on the early side.

Meters that arrive late

If the network or the screen is slow, a frame can arrive after its sound. No waiting can fix that. The calibration then asks the box to send frames sooner: it raises the box's margin by the lateness plus 20 ms, so frames land just early again, and this screen waits those 20 ms. The box can only take back what it was holding. If it was holding back less than the lateness, the remainder is the network or the screen and cannot be recovered.

The margin is one value for the whole box, so every screen gets its frames sooner. Screens that were already early just wait a little longer; with automatic recalibration on, they adjust by themselves. Otherwise, run their calibration again.

Tune with clicks: how the measurement works

The box stops the music and plays about 11 s of short, quiet tone bursts: 14 bursts of 8 ms at 1 kHz, −30 dBFS. They play through the normal path, in a separate MPD partition, so the music queue (and Qobuz Connect) is not touched. The music is not resumed afterwards. Meanwhile the phone's microphone records, and the page records when each meter frame arrives.

Each burst is found twice: as an onset in the microphone recording (a jump of 10 dB over the room's noise), and as an onset in the meter frames (a jump of 15 dB). The gaps between bursts are deliberately irregular (700, 530, 860, 610 ms, …), so only one time shift lines the two lists up. The page tries every shift, counts how many bursts pair up within ±40 ms, and keeps the best one. The median of those pairs is the measured offset.

The result is accepted when at least 9 of the 14 bursts pair up, and the best shift pairs at least 4 more than any other shift more than 100 ms away.

The two steps

1
Measuring. The bursts play with this screen's current delay. Frames are timed when they arrive. If they arrive before the sound, the new delay is that gap. If they arrive after it, the box is asked to send them sooner (see above). If step 1 fails, nothing is changed.
2
Checking. The bursts play again, and now frames are timed when they are drawn, this screen's delay included. What is measured is what is left over. Within ±30 ms counts as in sync.
↻
Check again repeats step 2. When the result is outside ±30 ms it corrects the delay (or, when the meters are late, the box) by what it found, and says so; check once more to confirm.

Without the OMDRC Android app there is no microphone: the clicks just play, and you set the delay with the ± buttons until the bars flick with each click.

Calibrate on the music, and automatic recalibration

Calibrate on the music does the same measurement without stopping anything. It listens for 10 s and compares the shape of the loudness in the microphone recording with the meters, at every shift from −600 to +2500 ms, and keeps the best match. Music with clear beats works best. A very regular beat can match in more than one place, and is then rejected as ambiguous. It tries up to 3 times.

Automatic recalibration (off by default) does this on Now playing when a track starts or playback resumes: 8 s at most every two minutes. The delay becomes the median of the last 3 good results. Failed, weak (match below 0.5) or late results are ignored. A blue light blinks over the meters while it listens.

When it fails

MessageWhat to do
the microphone heard only N of the 14 clicksRaise the volume a little, or hold the phone nearer the speakers.
the meters showed only N of the 14 clicksThe level stream did not reach this screen properly. Check the network and try again.
only N of 14 clicks lined upBoth saw the clicks, but the meter frames reached the screen too irregularly to pair them (usually a busy or sleepy Wi-Fi link). Try again. The log's arrival gaps show how irregular they were.
not holding them back at allThe frames arrive late even though the box sends them immediately. The network or the screen is slower than the whole sound path. Nothing can be taken back.

The Log button shows everything a run saw: the box's delay terms, every meter frame with its arrival time, the microphone envelope, the detected onsets, the candidate shifts and the verdict.
